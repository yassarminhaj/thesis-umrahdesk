The problem

Independent pilgrims are forced into a model built for big groups.

A pilgrim wants his own dates and his own family with him — not to wait for an agent's pre-purchased airline block and join a 100-member departure. I recently arranged exactly that for a family of six on their own terms — so I know it can work — but the current model still makes it the exception, not the norm.

The fixed-group package today

The hidden-inventory problem

Rooms sit empty while the board says "full."

Much of the accommodation around the Haram never reaches a public booking site. Large blocks are bought cheaply by big agents and resold privately — so a hotel can report itself full while rooms it has already sold sit unused. Independent pilgrims and smaller operators are quoted "sold out" against supply that physically exists.

The wedge — why this isn't just another OTA

The hard part isn't selling flights. It's the Masar pipeline.

Booking.com isn't the competitor — the manual chain is: documents, group formation, the Saudi operator, BRN approval, wallet crediting, and the visa request. UmrahDesk automates that pipeline and refuses to let an incomplete journey move forward.

The boundary that matters: UmrahDesk prepares, submits and tracks visa processing through authorized integrations. Actual issuance stays with the licensed government party — the platform automates the pipeline, it does not claim to issue the visa.
How the money moves

Paid straight to the supplier — the reference is the receipt.

There's no float to mismanage. The moment a component is chosen, its supplier is paid and the booking reference is realized on the spot. UmrahDesk keeps only its commission — everything else passes straight through.

Flights
PNR

Paid to the airline; the PNR is issued instantly.

Hotels
BRN

Paid to the hotel supplier; a booking reference comes straight back.

Visa
Elm wallet

Funds parked in the operator's Elm wallet; the Ministry of Interior debits it to issue the visa.

Transport & catering
Supplier a/c

Settled directly through integrated supplier accounts.

Commission stays with us; the rest flows through. Because a reference only exists once its supplier is paid, every confirmed leg is a real, funded booking — and an unpaid, unconfirmed component can't pose as a completed journey. UmrahDesk never sits on the customer's money.

Ground staff become reception, not a checkpoint. A large official workforce already meets every arrival. Today much of that effort goes into compliance checks and catching operator mistakes — essential work, but reactive, and it can make a pilgrim's first moments feel like an inspection rather than a welcome. Because an UmrahDesk group lands pre-coordinated — documented, expected and error-checked before the flight arrives — that same effort can shift toward receiving and guiding the group. The result is fewer penalty-triggering gaps for the operator, and a calmer, more dignified arrival for the pilgrim.
